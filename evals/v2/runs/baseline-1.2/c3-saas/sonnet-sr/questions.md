Good idea to start with a SaaS. Three quick choices first, so the plan fits your real customer and not a generic template. Reply with letters, or say "you decide" on any of them.

**Q1. Who is this for, and what one job should the first version finish?**
Why it matters: a SaaS for a plumber, a gym and a school looks completely different. The first release should finish one job well.
A. Small service businesses that send crews to job sites. Example: landscapers, cleaners or movers schedule who goes where, then bill for the work.
B. Small shops that take bookings from customers. Example: a salon where clients pick a time on their phone.
C. Something else. Tell me the customer and the job in one sentence.
My pick: A. It has a clear daily pain (a whiteboard or a group chat) and a clear payoff (getting paid faster).
You decide: delegate. Let me decide: keep it open.

**Q2. What stays out of this first release?**
Why it matters: scope is where first versions go wrong. Anything you leave out now cannot sneak into the screens, the database or the tickets.
A. Only the core job. Example: no payments inside the app, no customer portal, no route maps, no payroll.
B. Core job plus online card payments. Example: a customer pays an invoice by card from the email link.
C. Something else.
My pick: A. Payments add legal and money decisions that only you can make, and they can come in release 2.
You decide: delegate. Let me decide: keep it open.

**Q3. What should it look like?**
Why it matters: if you already have a brand, a Figma file or a design system, it wins over anything I propose.
A. I have my own design system or Figma file. Example: send me the link or the colors and fonts.
B. Propose a look for me. Example: a calm, dense, business-tool style that I show you first for a quick OK before any screens.
C. Something else.
My pick: B, unless you already have brand assets.
You decide: delegate. Let me decide: keep it open.
