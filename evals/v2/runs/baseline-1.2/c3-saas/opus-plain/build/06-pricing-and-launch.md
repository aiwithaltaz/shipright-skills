# Pricing, validation, and launch

## Pricing (flat per company, not per seat)

Small landscapers hate per-user pricing because crew size changes with the season. Charge by number of crews instead.

| Plan | Price | For | Includes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Starter** | $39/month | 1 crew, up to 5 people | Week board, crew app, invoicing, Stripe payments |
| **Crew** | $79/month | Up to 3 crews, 12 people | Everything in Starter, plus weather + rain move, reminders, QuickBooks sync |
| **Company** | $149/month | Up to 6 crews, 25 people | Everything in Crew, plus customer texts (fair-use limit), route order, priority support |

- 14-day free trial, no card needed.
- **Off-season pause**: $9/month from December to February keeps data and lets owners send invoices. This beats churn: without it, many will cancel every winter.
- Annual plan: 2 months free, billed in February (when they have cash from spring prepayments).
- Optional small fee on top of Stripe processing (e.g. 0.5%) is a later revenue lever. Don't start with it; it makes the pitch harder.

**Positioning against the market** (2026 list prices; verify before using in sales): Jobber Core starts around $49 and its crew-friendly tiers run $139 to $199+; Service Autopilot $49 to $499; landscape-specific suites like LMN and SingleOps start around $220 to $300 and go well past $500. Groundcrew sits at the low end on price but is built around crews and weeks, which the cheap tiers of general tools aren't.

## Validation before building more (weeks 0 to 2)

**Who to talk to:** owners of companies with 3 to 20 workers. Find them through local landscaping Facebook groups, r/landscaping and r/lawncare, state landscape associations, equipment dealers (they know every crew in town), and Google Maps.

**Questions to ask** (about the past, not hypotheticals):
1. Walk me through how you planned last week. What did you use?
2. What happened the last time it rained on a scheduled day?
3. When did you send invoices for work done last month? How long did it take?
4. What software have you tried? Why did you stop or why do you stay?
5. What do you pay for software today?

**Show them the week board prototype** and watch where they click. Ask: "What would you need to see here to use this on Monday?"

**Signals you're right:** they describe rain reshuffles and late invoicing as real pain without prompting; they ask when they can use it; at least 3 agree to pilot.
**Signals to change course:** they're happy with Jobber, or the real pain is estimating or hiring, not scheduling and billing.

## Launch plan

| When | Action |
|---|---|
| Pilots (3 to 5 companies) | Free for the first season in exchange for weekly feedback calls and a testimonial. |
| Pre-season (Jan to Mar) | Public launch. Landing page built around the rain-day demo. |
| Channels | Facebook groups and Reddit (helpful posts, not ads), local equipment dealers (referral fee), state landscape association booths, YouTube "how I run my lawn business" creators (sponsor small ones). |
| Content | "Rain day playbook", "How to invoice the same day the work is done", free invoice template that leads into the trial. |
| Referral | One free month for both companies per referral; owners know each other. |

## Metrics to watch

| Metric | Why |
|---|---|
| Trial to paid conversion | Target 20%+ for a niche tool with a clear demo. |
| Time to first scheduled week | Activation: if they don't plan a real week in 3 days, they won't convert. |
| % of visits marked done by crews (not the office) | Tells you if the crew app is actually used. |
| Days from visit done to invoice sent | The core promise. |
| Off-season pause vs cancel | Seasonal churn health. |

## Business risks

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Jobber is "good enough" for many | Win on crews + rain + price; target owners who left Jobber or never adopted software. |
| Seasonal churn | Off-season pause plan; annual billing in February. |
| Owners aren't at a desk | Week board must work on a phone and tablet from day one. |
| Low willingness to pay | Keep the $39 tier; show money recovered from visits that would have gone uninvoiced. |
