# Groundcrew: product brief

> Working name. Check the name and domain for trademark conflicts before you spend money on branding.

## One-line pitch

Plan the week's crews on one board, move a whole rain day in one tap, and turn finished visits into invoices on Friday afternoon.

## Who it's for

**Small landscaping and lawn-care companies with 3 to 20 workers** (usually 1 to 5 crews of 2 to 4 people).

| Person | What they do | What they need from the software |
|---|---|---|
| **Owner / operator** (the buyer) | Sells the work, plans the week, chases payments. Often still runs a crew some days. | See the whole week at a glance, reshuffle fast, get paid without an evening of paperwork. |
| **Office manager** (often a spouse or part-timer) | Answers calls, books jobs, sends invoices. | A calendar that doesn't fight them, invoices that fill themselves in. |
| **Crew lead** | Drives the truck, runs the day's route. | Today's list of stops on a phone, a way to mark a job done and add a note or photo. |

## The problem today

Most companies this size run on some mix of a whiteboard in the shop, a paper or Google calendar, group texts to crew leads, and QuickBooks or Word invoices typed up at the end of the month. That breaks in three predictable ways:

1. **Rain wrecks the plan.** One wet day means calling or texting every crew lead and re-writing the whole week by hand.
2. **Finished work doesn't become an invoice.** Visits get forgotten, invoiced late, or invoiced twice. Cash lags 30 to 60 days behind the work.
3. **Nobody knows who's overloaded.** The owner discovers on Thursday that Crew 2 has 11 hours of work booked into an 8-hour day.

## Why existing tools don't fit

The market has good tools, but they sit at two ends (prices are list prices seen in 2026 reviews; confirm before quoting them):

- **General field-service apps** (Jobber, Housecall Pro, Service Autopilot): roughly $49 to $199+/month. They're built around one technician visiting one job. Crews, recurring weekly routes, and weather are add-ons or workarounds.
- **Landscape-specific platforms** (LMN, SingleOps, Aspire): roughly $220 to $650+/month or custom quotes. Built for estimating, job costing, and 30+ person companies. Too heavy and too expensive for a 6-person outfit.

**The gap:** a crew-first, week-first scheduler with invoicing built in, priced for a company with one office person (or none).

## What makes Groundcrew different

1. **Crews are the unit, not individual workers.** You schedule "Crew 2 (Marco's truck)", not four separate people.
2. **The week board is the home screen.** Crews down the side, days across the top, load per crew per day shown in hours.
3. **Weather is built in.** The forecast sits on the board. When rain is coming, one action moves that day's jobs to the next open day and texts the affected customers.
4. **Done means billable.** Every visit marked done lands in a "Ready to invoice" list. Send them one by one, or batch the week in one click.

## Goals for the first 6 months

| Goal | Target |
|---|---|
| Paying companies | 40 |
| Weekly active (board opened 3+ days a week) | 70% of paying companies |
| Visits invoiced within 7 days of completion | 80% (from a likely baseline under 40%) |
| Time to set up a new company | Under 30 minutes, including importing customers |
| Monthly logo churn | Under 4% |

## Non-goals (on purpose)

- Detailed estimating and bid takeoffs (LMN owns this; integrate later if customers ask)
- Payroll and timesheets beyond simple clock-in/out
- Inventory, nursery, and materials tracking
- Big-company features: multiple branches, divisions, approval chains
