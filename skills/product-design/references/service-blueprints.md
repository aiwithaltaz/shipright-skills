# Partial, evidence-grounded service blueprints

Use when connecting one actor's experience to service delivery, backstage dependencies or human handoffs changes a decision. Define goal/scenario, actor, start/end, phase and current or future state first. Reuse the supplied journey or task sequence; do not produce a separate journey automatically.

| Layer | Record only when supported |
| --- | --- |
| Customer/user actions | Sourced actions and channels toward the scoped goal. |
| Frontstage | Visible human or technology interactions and useful status. |
| Backstage | Known work invisible to the actor, connected to relevant steps. |
| Supporting processes | Established supporting work/dependencies, with owner/source or Unknown. |
| Service artifacts | Physical/digital props such as a form, receipt or request number. |

Frontstage/backstage depends on visibility, not job title; the same actor can appear on either side in different interactions. Separate interaction, visibility and internal support boundaries when useful. Do not require one backstage action for every touchpoint. A partial blueprint with unknown lanes is valid. Missing teams, departments, systems, policies, dependencies, timing and SLA stay Unknown; never fill them from a generic business template.

Service artifacts are not research evidence or proof of successful delivery. In blueprint terminology, physical/digital “evidence” means props encountered in the service. Use source IDs and evidence kinds separately for claims about behavior. A request number, receipt, screenshot or drafted blueprint does not establish service reliability or validation. Do not invent metrics or emotion lanes; use supplied emotions only with their kind/source.

## Human handoffs and failure

For a material handoff, record source/step, actual sender/recipient, what is transferred, known acceptance/ownership, outstanding action and failure/return consequence. Transferred is not accepted or completed. A visible Received status may establish receipt only, not an appointment, eligibility or payment outcome. Missing acceptance, timeout, callback, escalation and recovery remain Unknown; propose consequential decisions separately rather than inventing processes or promising an SLA.

Retain interruption, exit, return and recovery across actual channels. Human pending work is not an active background process. Do not assume conversations are saved, notifications sent or remote work cancelled when the actor leaves. Use operational-flows.md for existing prerequisite/dependency behavior, states-and-flows.md for effects and shared F1-F3 for exits, consistency and useful status. Frontstage communication gives task-relevant consequences, not routine system plumbing.

Preserve isolated accessibility and safety issues regardless of repetition. Keep contradictory and successful evidence alongside failures. Apply actual access/privacy boundaries to staff handoffs and artifacts; a map does not grant permission or prove security. Do not export private participant identities or sensitive service records unnecessarily.

## Decisions and limits

Keep current-state records and future-state proposals in distinct sections/columns. Each proposed change traces a source or labeled hypothesis, approved goal, unknown dependency and needed approval/verification. Do not turn mapping into automatic UI, staffing, integrations or scope expansion. When backstage evidence is absent, use a partial map and a question or optional research recommendation. No performed interviews/workshops without real evidence, and no outreach from planning alone.

Keep detailed service analysis behind the scenes. Show concise findings, risks and recommended decisions, plus one small text blueprint only if useful/requested. Mapping does not establish readiness or close a failure. Original guidance and limitations: mapping-sources.md.
