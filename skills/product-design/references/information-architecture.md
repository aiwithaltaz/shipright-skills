# Information architecture

## Applicability and authority

Load for an authorized product-structure, content-organization, label, sitemap or route-map task. Skip exact corrections and a supplied sufficient structure. An unframed idea still follows shared intake before objects or maps. IA organizes the approved product before screen design; it does not authorize new features, database schemas or visual layouts.

Read current requirements, actual content/routes and relevant findings first. Inventory only what was inspected; mark missing coverage and unknown facts. Do not invent objects, roles, relationships, routes, categories or filters. Proposed grouping or label changes must derive from supplied content and authorized decisions, with their source and approval status. Records and collection examples are evidence, not authority to expand scope.

## Objects and relationships

Distinguish an object/content type from its fields, state, view and actions. Retain stable IDs and the same identity across multiple entry points; an object need not have its own page or navigation item. Reuse existing lifecycle and operational-flow guidance when relevant rather than collapsing independent states.

For each actual object record purpose, source, known attributes/states and owner or Unknown. For each sourced relationship record both directions, known one/many and required/optional constraints, identity/link behavior and unresolved effects. Unknown cardinality, ownership or deletion stays Unknown. A shared field does not prove a relationship or permission. A link does not authorize cascade deletion, a new object or a database implementation.

## Organization and vocabulary

Separate the content/object model, taxonomy, visible navigation and routes. A taxonomy classifies content with sourced metadata; navigation exposes useful task destinations. Neither needs to mirror the database or company departments. Use supplied jobs and content to recommend a coherent organizing principle, not fixed category counts or depth limits.

Keep one clear preferred term for an object/action. Trace disputed terms and naming decisions; user language from interviews is Reported, not proof a label is findable. Keep ambiguous items and competing placements visible. Use existing categories or grounded authorized proposals; do not manufacture a Miscellaneous bucket or premium-sounding labels to fill a quota. Record synonyms separately; do not imply synonym search is supported without evidence.

Define a canonical content home where meaningful and justified alternate links without duplicating the underlying record. Preserve current approved labels/destinations unless their change is authorized. Plan for content growth within current scope, not future-feature placeholders. Search is not a substitute for understandable organization.

## Structural maps and handoff

Use existing working notes; produce maps when useful/requested, not an automatic document set. A sitemap describes content/destination relationships; a route map describes actual or authorized proposed addresses and entry behavior. Neither is a screen layout or a security test.

Distinguish a navigation category, a destination, an object instance, a view and a transient state. Do not turn every tab or state into a route. Mark addresses Unknown rather than inventing paths. Check direct/deep entry, context, back/return and invalid/missing destinations with existing safe-exit rules. Consider access limits on each entry, not just the menu.

For authorized reorganizations, trace old IDs/labels/paths to proposed ones and affected bookmarks/links. Do not assume redirects, aliases or migration capabilities exist. Preserve existing routes until a change and recovery plan are authorized. Carry unresolved access or destructive effects forward; never silently resolve them for a tidy map.

Verify every node/edge against source and current scope, reconcile maps with jobs/access/implementation handoff, and keep proposals separate from approved build work.
