# Optional IA research

## Choose only when useful

Load when grouping, labels or findability uncertainty changes an authorized decision. Inspect available inventory, hierarchy and real evidence first. Skip sufficient evidence, exact corrections and tasks that do not need a study. Reuse Phase 1 method/planning rules for consent, access, privacy, resource constraints and study status. Plans stay unperformed unless real session evidence is available.

| Question | Optional method | Limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| How do relevant users group supplied items and describe groups? | Card sorting | Generates candidate groupings; does not deliver a final validated hierarchy |
| Can users locate a resource in an existing/proposed text hierarchy? | Tree testing | Evaluates labels/paths; does not establish full-interface usability or access enforcement |
| Do real navigation controls, search and access work together? | Relevant usability tasks with a usable artifact | Requires that artifact; a text tree cannot replace runtime/assistive-technology checks |

Do not require card sorting followed by tree testing for every product. Recommend the smallest useful next step and what would challenge the proposal. No fixed sample, card, category or depth quota guarantees validity. The study question and sampling/coverage constrain conclusions; expert or AI sorting is not participant research.

## Card sorting preparation

Use representative supplied content items within scope, not invented pages or future features. Use neutral, understandable card descriptions that avoid grouping through repeated keywords alone. Pilot materials when possible. Open sorting allows participant-generated groups/labels; closed sorting uses supplied categories; hybrid sorting mixes them and can bias alternatives. State why a variant fits the question rather than declaring a universal winner.

Let ambiguous or unrelated items remain ungrouped; retain disagreement and explanations. Proposed labels and groupings are candidates, not approved routes or proven user expectations. Do not manufacture participant names, groups, quotes, consensus or success percentages.

## Tree testing preparation

Use a known text hierarchy down to relevant destination(s), retaining its version, source and intended actor scope. Mark missing nodes Unknown rather than completing a plausible tree. Write realistic finding tasks without revealing the target label/path. Define intended destinations from the supplied structure, including multiple defensible answers when applicable. Identify direct success, detours, incorrect destination, abandonment and assistance if collected; no records means no metrics.

Pilot for understandable, neutral tasks and usable materials. Keep assignment and comparison effects visible when testing alternatives. Participant counts and apparent agreement cannot replace adequate sampling or explain causes. Qualitative records support bounded insights; population rates or superiority need appropriate measurement/design, never invented confidence.

## Analysis and limits

Reuse the optional synthesis guidance for record kinds, independent support, contradiction and source limits. Preserve isolated accessibility or safety issues regardless of repetition. Text-only tree tests omit interface cues, search and interactive accessibility; some tools constrain which nodes can count as answers. State such limitations before claiming coverage. Plan usable materials and relevant language/device/access contexts; do not equate a drag-and-drop card sort with accessible participation.

No tools/participants today: provide a useful proposed structure/task/plan with Unknowns and a disconfirming check, not validation. Recommendations authorize no recruitment, recording, payments, messages or installed analytics. Write only requested documents; users see a concise method recommendation, risks and next decision.
