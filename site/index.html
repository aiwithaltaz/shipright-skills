Why it looks better

Six decisions ShipRight made.

Each one is next to the part of the app it shaped.

  1. 01

    Two users, two sides.

    A salon has a client who wants to book fast and an owner who runs the day. ShipRight built a warm booking site for clients and a calm Today view for the owner. The plain build showed the booking panel inside the owner view too.

  2. 02

    References of the same kind.

    It looked at boutique studio and hotel booking sites, not software. An earlier version used tools like Linear and Stripe as references, and the salon came out looking like an admin tool.

  3. 03

    The mood came first.

    Before any screen, it picked a mood: a warm, quiet studio. The colors and photos come from that.

    • Paper cream #f3eee6
    • Copper #8f4334
    • Moss #234237
  4. 04

    Type with a voice.

    Fraunces, a soft serif, for headlines like "Book a chair." It feels human. Figtree for small text, so prices and times stay easy to read.

  5. 05

    Booking cut to the main job.

    Service, stylist, time, details. One step at a time, and the price is shown up front. Each finished step folds up so you can change it.

  6. 06

    The basics, done.

    One spacing scale. Clear focus outlines for keyboard users. A skip link. And an honest note on top that says it is a sample studio and nothing is sent.

How it works

Think first. Then design from real work.

1. The product

  • Who uses it, and why they open it today
  • What each user sees first
  • The 3 to 5 jobs that matter
  • Empty, loading and error states
  • Real looking sample data, labeled

2. The design

  • Name the product type first
  • Pick 2 or 3 great products of that type
  • Set a mood in three words
  • Build a small set of fonts, colors and spacing
  • Screenshot, compare, fix the weakest parts
