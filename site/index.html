How it works

Nine steps, in order.

The thinking comes before any pixels. Each step writes a short note the next one uses. The notes are short quotes from the real PRODUCT.md and DESIGN.md that ShipRight 1.6 wrote for the habit app test.

Step 1 · PRODUCT.md

Think about the product

  1. 01

    A basic idea

    You type one line. ShipRight turns it into a short paragraph: what it is, who pays, and the job it does every day.

    PRODUCT.md · IdeaFrom the habit run

    • “Nock is a pocket tally for one person.”
    • “They open it to mark today’s habits done, in one tap, and to see whether the week is holding.”
  2. 02

    Research

    It looks at the best products of the same type, what they do well, and where people get stuck.

    PRODUCT.md · ResearchFrom the habit run

    • Streaks: “a missed day resets that task’s streak to zero.”
    • Loop Habit Tracker: free and offline, “no official iOS version.”
    • A paper calendar “does not total a streak.”
  3. 03

    Define the users

    A main user and a second one. Their day, why they open the app, and what worries them.

    PRODUCT.md · UsersFrom the habit run

    • “One person with a handful of habits.”
    • “They get anxious when a counter snaps back to zero.”
  4. 04

    The PRD

    What each user sees first, the few jobs that matter, and what stays out of version one.

    PRODUCT.md · PRDFrom the habit run

    • “See today’s due habits and mark one done, or undo that mark, in one tap.”
    • Stays out: “accounts, sync, notifications, widgets”
  5. 05

    Flows and states

    The 3 to 5 main jobs in a few clicks each, plus empty, loading and error states, and real looking sample data.

    PRODUCT.md · Flows and statesFrom the habit run

    • “Tap Notch on a due habit. Tap Done on that same habit to undo.”
    • Empty: “No habits yet, plus Add a habit and Load sample data”
    • Failed: “Storage blocked... Try again.”

Steps 2 to 4 · DESIGN.md

Design from real references, build, then look and fix

  1. 06

    References and brand mood

    It names the product type, picks 2 or 3 great references of that type, sets a mood in three words, and tests that the look is not the usual AI look.

    DESIGN.md · References and moodFrom the habit run

    • Streaks, Things 3, Loop Habit Tracker
    • Brand mood: “Index card, ballpoint, notch.”
    • Genericness test: “A cream page, a high-contrast serif, and a terracotta accent would look like any other generated app.”
  2. 07

    Design system

    Two fonts, a short type scale, one spacing scale and about ten colors, all in one file.

    Type: Bricolage Grotesque + Source Sans 3

    4 of 5 still open today

    Walk outside for 20 minutes

    Every day · 7 days in a row

    Spacing

    4
    8
    16
    32
    48

    Colors

  3. 08

    Build the UI

    Every screen is built to sit next to its references, with short, real copy.

  4. 09

    Look and fix

    A fresh critic looks at desktop and phone screenshots, ignoring the plan. It fixes the worst problems, then checks for broken things.

How we test: same prompt, same model (Grok 4.7 in Grok Build), one run each. We grade both apps ourselves with our rubric, so it is not blind. The salon and CRM runs used the reference-first method before it was released; the habit run used ShipRight 1.6. The habit craft win is close: 29 vs 28. Earlier versions with more rules scored lower on craft than no skill at all, which is why ShipRight 1.6 is lean.
