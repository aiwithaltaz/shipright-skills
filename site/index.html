Existing app

It uses every screen like a first-time user. It says what works first, then lists each pain with a screenshot and a cause, and ranks the fixes.

01-prd.md

Who: the owner or office manager of a landscaping company with 3 to 20 workers, usually 1 to 4 crews.

Job: put every job on a crew and a day without overloading anyone, then bill for finished jobs.

What they do today: Unknown. Hypothesis H1: a whiteboard, a group chat, a spreadsheet or paper. Source needed: 5 owner conversations.

Unknown stays Unknown. It names who checks it.

02-technical-architecture.md

LayerChoiceSwitch when
AppNext.jsHeavy background jobs appear
Data and sign-inSupabaseStrict data residency
EmailResendReminders or marketing mail

Not added: analytics, payments, maps, queues, a separate API server.

Every choice has a reason and a switch point.

04-frontend-spec.md

S1 Schedule. Job: see the week by crew and place jobs.

Load line: "6.5 of 8 h". Over: "9.5 of 8 h, over by 1.5 h" in amber.

States: populated, empty week, loading, error. 0, 1 and many jobs per cell.

Do not add: drag and drop, filters, charts, summary cards, notification dots.

Each screen has a job, its states, and what to leave out.

05-feature-ticket-list.md

T4 Schedule board, happy path. Goal: see a week by crew.

Done when: 0, 1 and 12 jobs per cell render at 1280. Enter opens the job panel. The week in the URL survives reload.

Do not add: drag and drop, filters.

One prompt per ticket. Paste it into your coding tool.

It reads 6 cards, not 64 websites.

We read 64 public sources on UX, research, architecture, design systems and motion, and turned them into short rule cards. Each card keeps its source, a short quote and a check.

A small script deals 5 to 8 cards that fit your stage and project. The rest of the library never enters the chat, so the AI does not skim.

We test it against Opus. Here is where it lost.

Same prompt. Opus alone on the left. Sonnet with ShipRight 1.2 on the right. Opus thought harder about the product. These three pairs are why 1.3 exists.

LinkedIn assistant

Opus led with the situation: 7 actions left, about 13 minutes, everything drafted. The 1.2 screen showed users a "Preview state" switch meant for developers.

AI agent dashboard

Opus put the fix on each problem, like Pause agent and Cap at $150/day, and compared each number to normal: 18%, usually 2%. The 1.2 screen listed statuses with no action.

Landscaping SaaS

Both flagged the crew day over its hours. Opus also put the week's money and Thursday's rain in the headline, with one button to move 9 visits.

What 1.3 changed because of it

  1. Situation headline. The title states what is going on and the next decision, with numbers.
  2. The fix sits on the item. Each row that needs attention carries its one action.
  3. Compare to normal. A number shows its usual value beside it.
  4. No dev controls. Review switches hide behind ?review=1.
  5. Sample is allowed, if labeled. Rich demo data, never passed off as real.

1.3 eval runs are prepared in the repo and not graded yet. No score is shown until one exists.
